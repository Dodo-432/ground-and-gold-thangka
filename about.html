About Ground & Gold

The name comes from two things every thangka depends on: the prepared ground the painter works on, and the gold that brings the finished image to life.

Why craft

Most writing about thangkas focuses on meaning: who the deity is and what the symbols represent. That matters, and other places cover it well. We wanted a space for the other half of the story, the material and visual decisions that make a painting good or ordinary.

Who writes here

Ground & Gold is a small independent project run by people who love looking at Himalayan painting. We are not painters, dealers or scholars, and we do not pretend to be. Our notes are careful summaries of how the craft is widely described, written to help readers look more closely.

What we will never do

We do not appraise, authenticate or sell paintings, publish paid reviews, or make up numbers to sound authoritative. If something is uncertain or varies between workshops, we say so.

About the images

Images on this site are historical works from The Metropolitan Museum of Art's Open Access program, released under CC0. Captions give each work's title and approximate date.
